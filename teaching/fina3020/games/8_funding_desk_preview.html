Your job: choose the funding

You run treasury for a US parent with euro operations. Each mandate needs €10M for one year. The investment decision has already been made. Your job is to minimize the euro repayment while meeting the borrowing limits.

Each mandate receives €10.375M at maturity from the business: €10M for principal and €375,000 for financing. Any surplus goes into your cash reserve; any shortfall comes out of it. You start with €250,000. Each loan matures before the next mandate begins. Your reserve carries forward.

These are classroom quotes. All rates are USD per EUR. The business supplies the euro repayment; the swap fixes how many euros the USD debt will require. Both routes have the same one-year maturity.

A small worked example

You need €1M. Spot is 1.10, the USD loan rate is 5%, the forward is 1.12, and the swap fee is €5,000, paid at maturity.

TodayBorrow $1,100,000Sell dollars to receive €1M.
In one yearOwe $1,155,000Include principal and 5% interest.
Buy dollars forwardPay €1,036,250$1,155,000 ÷ 1.12 + €5,000.

The euro funding cost is 3.625%: (€1,036,250 ÷ €1M) − 1. Compare that with the euro loan rate. You will need both the spot conversion and the forward repayment in every mandate.

Keep the cash-flow formula handy

For €x funded through the swap: USD borrowed = x × spot ask. USD repayment = x × spot ask × (1 + USD loan rate). Euro repayment = USD repayment ÷ forward bid + swap fee.

The fee in this game is 0.5% of the euros funded through the swap, paid at maturity. It is zero when you use only the local loan. The reserve earns no interest and stays separate from the €10M funding requirement.
