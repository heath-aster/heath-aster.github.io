Follow the money around the triangle

Each quote is quote currency per 1 unit of base currency. The dealer buys the base at the bid and sells the base at the ask.

Set each FX midpoint, then change its spread independently.

Where does the equation come from?

Substitute each leg’s output into the next leg. The currency units cancel, leaving a multiplier: final HKD ÷ starting HKD.

Subtract 1 to remove your original money. Multiply by 100 to express the return as a percent. No arbitrage requires both route multipliers to be at most 1.

Why does the next slide cap the trade at HK$750?

The three-row books above use illustrative depth large enough for the HK$1,000 walkthrough; their extra levels are not used in this calculation. The slide’s separate depth example has much smaller quantities. A larger trade would need worse prices, so the best-price return would no longer apply to the entire amount.

Made-up classroom quotes. All three trades are assumed to execute at the displayed best prices, with sufficient depth and no additional fees or price changes. Calculations retain full precision; displayed amounts are rounded.
