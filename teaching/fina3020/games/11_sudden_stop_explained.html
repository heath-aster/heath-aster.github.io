Emerging-Market Hedge Fund: student explanation

Open the activity

The fund’s decision

You manage an emerging-market hedge fund with USD 100 million of net asset value (NAV). NAV is assets minus liabilities. Choose a bond book for the next quarter and keep enough cash to meet investor withdrawals.

Buy local-currency sovereign bonds or USD sovereign bonds. Keep the residual in USD cash. You may borrow up to USD 50m, sell part of the local-currency principal forward and buy a credit default swap (CDS) on the USD sovereign bonds. A CDS pays under the stated covered credit event.

You choose the portfolio. The authorities’ response is a scenario condition. The same book faces stabilization, a sudden stop and restructuring. Each case starts from the same USD 100m NAV. These are independent scenarios with no estimated probabilities.

Investment risk

Local bonds combine local returns and FX changes. USD bonds remove direct local-currency exposure but retain sovereign credit risk. Hedges cost cash and cover specified risks.

Cash timing

Investors ask for cash before the FX and CDS contracts settle. Segregated collateral stays blocked at that checkpoint. A fund can have positive NAV and still need to sell assets at a discount.

First follow the example, practise seven calculations and check a simulated AI memo. Then choose one book, compare all three cases and explain the tradeoff. Hints, errors and market losses do not reduce participation credit.

Example: positions, hedges and cash

Start with USD 40m of local bonds, USD 40m of USD sovereign bonds and USD 20m of cash. Borrow nothing. Hedge USD 20m of local-currency principal and buy USD 20m of CDS protection. Each hedge covers 50% of its corresponding initial bond position. All amounts below are USD millions.

  1. Prefund costs: FX hedge cost = 1% × 20 = 0.20. Quarterly CDS premium = 0.75% × 20 = 0.15. Total cost K = 0.35.
  2. Segregate collateral: Q = 10% × 20 + 5% × 20 = 3.00. Cash available before settlement = 20 − 0.35 − 3 = 16.65. Collateral remains a fund asset.
  3. Translate local bonds in the sudden stop: 40 × (1 − 8%) × (1 − 15%) = 31.28. The total USD return is −21.80%. The FX hedge later pays −20 × (−15%) = 3.00.
  4. Meet the cash request: sudden-stop redemptions are 20.00. The cash deficit is 20 − 16.65 = 3.35. At a 5% sale discount, sell marked bonds of 3.35 / 0.95 = 3.53 to raise 3.35. The extra loss is 0.18.
  5. Calculate investment NAV: 20 − 0.35 + 31.28 + 35.20 + 3 − 0.176315789 = 88.95. This implies an investment return of −11.05%. After paying 20 of redemptions, remaining NAV is 68.95.

Investment NAV shows performance before investor withdrawals. Remaining NAV deducts the withdrawals actually paid. Both account for the forced-sale discount. Cash proceeds from a sale replace assets sold. They are never added as new profits.

Example investment NAV before withdrawals: stabilization 103.49, sudden stop 88.95, restructuring 79.79. Returns: +3.49%, −11.05%, −20.21%. Available checkpoint cash is 16.65 in each case. Extra sale losses: 0.00, 0.18 and 2.36.

In restructuring, the USD bond’s −40% return already represents 60% recovery. A covered CDS pays 40% of protected notional at quarter end. The model does not add a second bond default loss.

Checking a simulated AI analyst memo

The fund uses an AI assistant to extract reserve and debt figures. This fixed classroom case shows a plausible error. All figures below describe a fictional country on 31 October 2026. The country’s 30-day funding window is separate from the fund’s quarterly investment horizon.

Draft memo: “Gross reserves are USD 20bn and external payments due are USD 12bn. The USD 8bn buffer makes it safe for the fund to add unhedged local bonds.”

Source A: central-bank reserve note

Gross reserves: USD 20bn. Encumbered reserves unavailable for these payments: USD 6bn. Additional forward dollar deliveries due over the next 30 days: USD 5bn. These deliveries are separate from the USD 6bn encumbered amount.

Source B: external financing schedule

Other external payments due over the next 30 days: USD 12bn. This figure excludes the USD 5bn of forward dollar deliveries in Source A. Both sources cover the same fictional country and reporting date.

Source correction and investment judgment

After excluding encumbered reserves and the additional forward deliveries, available reserves for the other payments are 20 − 6 − 5 = USD 9bn. The USD 12bn of other payments therefore leaves a USD 3bn gap. The two deductions do not overlap, and the payment schedule excludes the forward deliveries. This prevents double counting.

The draft used gross reserves as though every dollar were available. Its USD 8bn buffer is the wrong funding comparison. These sources support a liquidity concern. A trade still requires refinancing assumptions, asset prices, policy credibility and fund risk limits. The human analyst checks source definitions, reporting dates, units and scope before approving a position.

Model, units and limits

All money amounts are USD millions. Initial NAV is 100. A = local bonds, B = USD sovereign bonds, L = borrowing, H = FX principal hedged, P = CDS notional. H ≤ A, P ≤ B and 0 ≤ L ≤ 50. Returns below are decimal changes over one quarter. Both bonds start at par, so entry investment equals initial principal valued in USD.

Stabilization: local return +3%, FX +3%, USD bonds +5%, redemptions10, sale discount0%. Sudden stop: −8%, −15%, −12%, redemptions20, discount5%. Restructuring: −15%, −25%, −40%, redemptions30, discount15%.

Residual cash, costs and collateral
C = 100 + L − A − B. K = .01H + .0075P + .015L. Q = .10H + .05P. Entry requires C ≥ K + Q. Available checkpoint cash = C − K − Q. K prefunds all quarterly charges. Cash earns zero in this exercise.
Marked bond values
Local mark = A(1 + local return)(1 + FX return). FX return means the change in the USD value of one local-currency unit. USD bond mark = B(1 + USD bond return). Each bond return includes income and its price change. M is their sum. All scenario bond and FX repricing occurs by the checkpoint. Bond marks stay fixed until derivatives settle later at quarter end.
Gross derivative settlements at quarter end
FX payoff = −H × FX return. It hedges initial principal. The simplified payoff uses the initial spot value as its reference and separately charges the stated 1% hedge cost. CDS pays .40P only in restructuring. That case assumes a covered credit event, 60% recovery and performance by the counterparty. Other cases value the CDS settlement at zero.
Checkpoint redemptions and forced-sale loss
Investors request R = 10, 20 or 30 before derivatives settle. Collateral stays blocked until quarter end. S = max(0, R − available cash). Sell marked bonds V = min(M, S/(1 − k)) pro rata, where the sale discount k is 0, .05 or .15. Proceeds = (1 − k)V. Extra sale loss = kV. Unmet checkpoint redemptions = max(0, S − proceeds).
Investment performance and remaining fund NAV
Investment NAV before withdrawals = C − K + M − L + FX payoff + CDS payout − kV. This is quarter-end remaining NAV plus investor withdrawals paid, so withdrawals do not count as investment losses. Investment return (%) = 100 × (investment NAV / 100 − 1). Paid redemption = R − unmet redemption. Remaining NAV = investment NAV − paid redemption. Collateral is already inside C and adds no second asset. Sale proceeds replace sold assets and create no separate gain.

Timing: the cash test concerns payment at an earlier checkpoint. Investment NAV concerns value after quarter-end settlement, measured before investor withdrawals. Positive NAV does not establish that earlier cash obligations can be met. An unmet redemption is a liquidity shortfall at the checkpoint, not a statement of insolvency. All feasible books in the current three cases can meet the full redemption request through available cash and bond sales. The cash deficit before sales and the sale loss show the liquidity cost.

Limits: all coefficients, prices, redemptions and policy descriptions are teaching assumptions. There is no probability estimate or recommended trade. The model omits variation margin changes, capital controls on payments, refinancing of fund debt, counterparty failure, basis risk, taxes and bond-specific CDS settlement details. Borrowing stays outstanding until quarter end. Bond marks stay fixed from the sale checkpoint to quarter-end settlement. Hedges remain outstanding at their initial notionals after a forced sale, so protection can exceed the remaining bond exposure. Official responses are scenario conditions rather than choices by the student.
