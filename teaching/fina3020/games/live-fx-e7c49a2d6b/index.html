ONE ROUND = ONE YEAR

Where the numbers come from

All dollar amounts are in domestic currency. For convenience, today’s spot rate is S₀ = $1 per unit of foreign currency. Let x be its percentage change over the next year, so the maturity spot rate is S₁ = S₀ × (1 + x). A negative x means the foreign currency depreciates.

The classroom assumes a 0% domestic annual interest rate and a 2% foreign annual interest rate. Covered interest parity therefore sets the one-year forward rate F = S₀ × 1.00 / 1.02 = $0.980392. Selling forward locks in a 1.9608% discount to today’s spot value. The rates and $100,000 amounts are teaching assumptions; the instructor chooses the shock probabilities.

The exporter’s receivable and the investment portfolio are two separate $100,000 exercises. Their allocation percentages do not need to add to 100%. Each new round starts with fresh amounts at the same initial spot rate; cumulative P&L simply adds each year’s result.

01 · TRADE EXPOSURE

An exporter’s receivable

You will receive 100,000 foreign-currency units in one year, worth $100,000 at today’s spot rate. Hedge share h by selling it one year forward; exchange the rest at the maturity spot rate.

0% · All exposed100% · All hedged

Trade P&L = $100,000 × [h × (1 / 1.02 − 1) + (1 − h) × x]

02 · FINANCIAL EXPOSURE

Cash or foreign carry?

Invest share a of $100,000 in a one-year foreign deposit at 2% annual interest. Keep the remainder in domestic cash at 0%. Convert the foreign principal and interest back at maturity. No borrowing.

0% · All cash100% · All foreign

One-year return in domestic currency = 1.02 × (1 + x) − 1. For example, a −8% FX shock gives 1.02 × 0.92 − 1 = −6.16%, including the interest earned.

Financial P&L = $100,000 × a × [1.02 × (1 + x) − 1]
