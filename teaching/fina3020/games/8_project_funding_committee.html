2. A host-country policy shock

New remittance rules, announced before any forward is signed

The Year 2 cash flow of EUR 6.4M faces a 20% withholding tax and a one-year freeze. The frozen cash earns 3% locally and reaches the parent in Year 3. Years 1 and 3 are unchanged.

  • Case A, no usable credit: the withholding tax is a cash loss. EUR in Year 3 = 6.4 × 0.80 × 1.03.
  • Case B, full usable credit: the parent uses the full credit against home tax it would otherwise pay in that year, so the withholding costs the group nothing. EUR in Year 3 = 6.4 × 1.03.

Your decision if no credit is usable (Case A)

What this means

Only cash that reaches the parent counts. Replace the old Year 2 PV with the new Year 3 PV: convert at the Year 3 rate and discount three years. The credit removes the tax loss. The one-year delay still costs value when local cash earns less than the parent's 10% required return.

What this means

EUR repayment = USD repayment / F. Hedge the full USD repayment, principal and interest. The swap route can be cheaper when the parent borrows on better terms than the subsidiary. The forward already prices the currency difference.

The parent now borrows enough USD to buy EUR 10M at the spot ask. Recompute the all-in EUR cost of the swap route.

How does the funding choice enter the project valuation?

What this means

The spot ask raises the USD the parent must borrow. The forward bid raises the EUR needed to buy the USD repayment. Mid rates can show a saving that executable quotes remove.
