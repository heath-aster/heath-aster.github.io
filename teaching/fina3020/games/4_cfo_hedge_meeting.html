Breakeven Intuition: Raising the crash probability shifts weight from the two stronger states onto ST = 7.80 while keeping their 2:1 ratio. Once the crash probability passes 25.60%, the locked forward beats staying unhedged in expectation as well as in certainty. Below it you are paying a small expected-revenue premium for a known number.
Core Misconception Debrief & Lessons:
  • Misconception 1 (Wrong Option Type): If you bought a Call Option for a receivable, you paid an upfront premium for the right to buy Euros at 8.40 when you needed to sell Euros. You suffered the full spot drop to 7.80 PLUS the lost premium!
  • Forward vs Option Trade-Off: In the ECB-cut state the Forward lock yields HKD 8,420,000 (fully protected at zero upfront cost), while the Put Option floor yields HKD 8,278,500 (exercised at the 8.40 strike, less the 0.1215 premium FV). Protection through the option costs HKD 141,500 more, which is the price of keeping the upside.
  • When is the Option Better? The Put Option outperforms the Forward only if the Euro rallies above ST > F0 + pfv = 8.4200 + 0.1215 = 8.5415 HKD/EUR.

Hedging is not all-or-nothing. Split the €1,000,000 receivable across a forward sleeve, a put option sleeve and an open sleeve, and watch the payoff curve rebuild itself.

Reading the curve
  • The forward sleeve is a flat line. It contributes the same HKD at every ST, which is why adding forward flattens the whole curve.
  • The open sleeve is a 45-degree line through the origin. It sets how steeply you fall when the euro falls.
  • The put sleeve is flat below K = 8.40 and 45-degree above it. That is the kink: your mix has slope 0% below the strike and 0% above it.
  • Only the open and put sleeves earn anything from a euro rally, and only the forward and put sleeves protect you from a euro crash. Moving the sliders is how you price that trade-off against your own view of f(ST).
